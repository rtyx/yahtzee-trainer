# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Casual Yahtzee players who want to get better without feeling like they are studying a spreadsheet. They are usually playing in short sessions, learning by doing, and using the trainer to understand why one keep or scoring choice is stronger than another. They care about maximizing score, but they need the path to optimal play to feel approachable and calm rather than intimidating.

## Product Purpose
Yahtzee Trainer helps players practice optimal Yahtzee decisions in a way that is readable, tactile, and easy to trust. It turns each turn into a small lesson: what to keep, what to score, and how far the chosen move was from the best move. Success means players can build intuition for stronger choices, improve accuracy over time, and feel like they are using a refined tabletop tool rather than a flashy game app.

## Brand Personality
Approachable, paper-like, disciplined. The product should feel orderly, tactile, and quietly confident, with a Swiss-influenced sense of structure and spacing. It should welcome non-expert players while still signaling strategic rigor and care.

## Anti-references
Do not make this feel like a casino interface, a mobile game with gimmicks, or a generic SaaS dashboard. Avoid loud color, decorative effects, cluttered surfaces, novelty interactions, and anything that makes the trainer feel toy-like or unserious. The UI should not drift into glossy gamification or abstract data-tool aesthetics that undermine the paper score-sheet metaphor.

## Design Principles
Practice through clarity. Every screen should make the next best action obvious, so learning happens through repeated use rather than explanation overload.

Physical over flashy. Preserve the feeling of a real score sheet and realistic dice, using spacing, borders, and structure to create trust instead of decorative effects.

Discipline in color. The interface should rely primarily on black, white, and nuanced grays, with green and red reserved for performance feedback and other meaning-bearing moments.

Swiss rhythm, human tone. Layout, margins, padding, and typography should feel precise and intentional, but never cold or forbidding to casual players.

Teach optimal play without punishing curiosity. Feedback should be honest about better decisions while keeping the experience encouraging and easy to continue.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Target WCAG AA contrast in both light and dark mode. Do not rely on color alone to communicate whether a decision was optimal or not; pair color with copy, icons, or clear labels. Keep realistic dice highly legible, with strong pip contrast and shapes that remain readable across themes. Preserve comfortable spacing, touch targets, and visual hierarchy for casual use. If motion is introduced later, it should remain restrained and respect reduced-motion preferences.
